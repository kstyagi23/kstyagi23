## Working Materials

**Languages:** Python, TypeScript / JavaScript, Rust.  
**AI & data:** PyTorch, ONNX Runtime, quantization, RAG, vector search, graph storage.  
**Shipping:** FastAPI, Flask, Node.js, PostgreSQL, MongoDB, Redis, Docker, Linux.

I care about what happens after the happy-path demo.

When the internet dies. When the context gets too large. When the process restarts and forgets everything. When the hardware has less memory than the model would really like.

Or when someone uses the keyboard instead of the mouse and discovers the entire interface was apparently held together by hope.

The happy path is lovely. Unfortunately, production has other plans.

## Human Runtime

### The person behind the commit history.

I'm more *take the bike out and see where the road goes* than *stand in a crowded room pretending to enjoy networking*.

I like machines you can tinker with, software that feels thoughtfully made, analog things that don't need firmware updates, and projects that begin with "this shouldn't be too hard" and end with a new directory full of experiments.

Mountains are a good excuse to disappear for a while. So are interesting engineering problems. Both have a habit of making you underestimate how long you'll be gone.

A few things that give you a better idea of the person behind the repositories:

- **Analog watches over smartwatches.** I like knowing the time without needing to update anything.
- **Motorcycles, roads, and mountains.** Especially roads that lead somewhere worth seeing. The risk assessment sometimes involves a longer conversation with my family than the trip itself.
- **Machines with a little character.** I own an RC Dodge Charger monster truck. Not every machine needs to be useful. Some just need to be ridiculously fun.
- **Unnecessarily ambitious side projects.** [A GPU-powered calculator](https://github.com/kstyagi23/calc-on-gpu), because apparently `2 + 2` needed a graphics pipeline.
- **Things that work without asking permission from the cloud.** Local compute, offline capability, and software that doesn't fall apart the moment the network does.
- **Substance over ceremony.** I'd rather build something interesting than spend all day talking about building something interesting.
- **Not particularly into clubbing.** Give me a good road, an interesting machine, or a problem I can get lost in. I'll be entertained for hours, probably longer than is healthy.

Things I have little patience for? Complexity that serves no purpose, interfaces that make simple things difficult, and systems that promise the world right until you try using them.

I also have a weakness for ideas that are probably more ambitious than they need to be. This is both a personality trait and an ongoing operational concern.

Still interested in building a TERMINATOR. The legal kind.

Preferably one that doesn't need a cloud subscription.
